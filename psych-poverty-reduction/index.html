Aspiring to a Better Future: Can a Simple Psychological Intervention Reduce Poverty?

Kate Orkin, Robert Garlick, Mahreen Mahmud, Richard Sedlmayr, Johannes Haushofer, Stefan Dercon

The Review of Economic Studies

Read on the Review of Economic Studies site NBER working paper (2023)

Summary

A field experiment in Kenya testing whether a short aspirations-and-planning workshop can change economic outcomes for people living in poverty. The Agency Fund's graduation coaching report describes the findings this way:

Meanwhile, in Kenya, an eighty-minute aspirations-and-planning workshop raised productive investment, revenue, consumption and asset value at seventeen months, and nearly paid for itself in consumption gains alone over that period (Orkin et al., 2023). Notably, the same trial finds that the workshop combined with a large cash transfer produced effects similar to the cash transfer alone, suggesting that improving economic conditions can activate the same aspirational process.

Quoted from What is Graduation Coaching? (Khan and Andrews-Fearon, The Agency Fund, draft August 2026). For the authors' own abstract, see the Review of Economic Studies page.
