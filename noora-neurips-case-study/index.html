Evaluating the Early Deployment of a GenAI Co-Pilot for Frontline Maternal and Newborn Health Communication: A Mixed-Methods Case Study from India

From the paper

This is a two-page extended abstract. The passages below are quoted from it.

Health systems in low- and middle-income countries are beginning to use generative AI (GenAI) to help frontline workers manage high volumes of incoming messages [7]. Because these tools often reach patients through human workers, evaluations should examine how GenAI affects workers’ judgment, communication practices, and agency, and the services patients receive.

The study asks two questions. RQ1: How do MSEs with co-pilot access experience the tool, including changes in attitudes, trust, agency, and practice, and what do MSEs without access expect from it? RQ2: Are conversations handled by co-pilot MSEs measurably different in interactional dynamics, informational content, and rubric-rated quality, and do they match MSEs’ reported experiences?

For AI evaluation in the Global South, our case study makes two contributions. First, it offers a practical example for evaluating human-in-the-loop GenAI in multilingual settings where ground-truth labels are scarce and outcomes depend on frontline workers’ judgments. Second, it also shows how evaluators can triangulate workers’ reported experience against behavioral traces in service data, testing whether attitudes toward GenAI match changes in communication.

MSEs are the service's Medical Support Executives, who answer patient and caregiver messages.
